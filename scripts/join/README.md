# Joining an existing chain

Two scripts, in order.

```bash
./01-install-node.sh <moniker> <peer-rpc-url> [peer-id@host:port ...]
# start the node, let it catch up, then generate the operator key in a ceremony
./02-apply-to-join.sh
```

Step 1 makes you a **full node**. Step 2 makes you an **applicant**. Neither
makes you a validator: admission is a governance vote, and the ante handler
rejects `create-validator` from an unapproved account before it is processed.

The sequence exists in that order because it is the argument. You are asking to
be trusted with block production, and having demonstrably kept up is the case
you bring.

## What step 2 asks for that older versions did not

The legal entity, the ultimate beneficial owner, and the jurisdiction. Those
three are what let concentration be bounded by arithmetic — per entity, per
owner, per jurisdiction, checked every epoch — rather than by somebody having to
refuse a government's application, which is a diplomatic act that reads as
exclusion.

The chain cannot detect a false declaration. It makes one documented, signed by
your own key, and actionable.

## Two keys, opposite handling

The **consensus key** is generated by `init` on the node and must never leave it.
The **operator key** comes from a ceremony, keeps its mnemonic on paper, and on a
deployment holding value should not live on the node at all.

Full detail: [Run a validator](../../docs/guides/validator.md), and
[Launching a chain](../../docs/guides/launch.md) if you are starting one rather
than joining.
